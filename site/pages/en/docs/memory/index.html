Everyday use

Memory and the workspace.

An agent's memory isn't hidden inside a model. It's a set of pages in its workspace, which you can open, read and edit, in RedLeaf or as ordinary files on your disk. When something is wrong, you fix the page and the agent works from your version.

What the workspace is

Each agent's Workspace field points at a page. Everything under that page is the agent's workspace: its memory, its configuration and the files it keeps. Agents read it at the start of a discussion and write to it as they learn, so a brand-new discussion next week starts where this one ended.

The same pages are also a real folder. While RedLeaf runs, every workspace appears as files you can open with any app:

PlatformWhere
WindowsL:\Workspaces\<agent>, through WinFsp
Linux~/Leaf/Workspaces/<agent>, through FUSE 3

Each page is a Markdown file there. Every file is backed by a page and its history, so a change made in a text editor shows up in RedLeaf, and the other way round. Drop a document into the folder and the agent can read it, sort it, or file it.

Your own projects as folders

The projects you create in Workspace are folders on the same drive, next to the agents' workspaces:

PlatformWhere
WindowsL:\Projects\<project>
Linux~/Leaf/Projects/<project>

They work like a workspace: pages are Markdown files, edits go both ways and keep their history, and you can keep images and other files there too. Create, rename or delete a project in Workspace and its folder follows within a second, while RedLeaf runs. A project that is an agent's workspace appears once, under Workspaces.

Anything on the drive can read these folders, so confidential projects and confidential pages are left out of it. Open those in RedLeaf.

Reading and correcting memory

  1. Open the workspaceIn Workspace, open the agent's project, for example Nova. Or open its folder on your disk.
  2. Find the pageThe sidebar shows the page tree under Structure, what changed lately under Recent, and linked items under Entities.
  3. Edit itChange the text like any document. If you'd rather not, just tell the agent in a discussion: “That's wrong, the deadline moved to Friday. Fix it everywhere.”

Every page keeps its history. Open it to see Recent activity, who changed what, and Undo this change or revert to an earlier version.

The Journal

Next to an agent's Live discussion, the Journal tab is its diary: a day-by-day record of what it did, split into morning, afternoon and evening. Filter by With you, On my own, Scheduled or Failed, and jump from any entry to the discussion or message behind it. The Journal is read only. It records what happened; memory is what the agent chose to keep.

Overnight: the dream cycle

Agents with the dreaming skills, Nova included, run a short cycle every night at 4 in the morning:

  • Dreaming goes over the day, keeps the facts and decisions worth keeping, resolves contradictions with what was already there, and rebuilds the memory index.
  • Emotional dreaming writes a small mood note that colours the next day's tone.
  • Creative dreaming writes down ideas for your projects, clearly marked as proposals and not decisions.

Each run appears in the agent's Journal and in Compute › Jobs. If you change the cycle's workflow, RedLeaf leaves your version alone.

Scratch space

Agents also get a disposable scratch area for downloads, experiments and temporary files: drive S: on Windows. Anything left there is deleted after 30 days of inactivity. Change that in Settings › Storage › Scratch. Anything worth keeping belongs in the workspace.
