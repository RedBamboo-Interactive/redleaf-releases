Everyday use

Nova and your own agents.

An agent is a character with a job: a name, a personality, the AI provider that does its thinking, a workspace for its memory, and the skills it may use. Nova is the first one. You can make as many as you like.

Nova

Setup creates Nova for you: a warm, capable assistant with a starting personality, an avatar, a set of skills, the provider you chose, and a workspace called Nova. She starts from that template and builds her relationship with you from there, in her memory. Everything about her can be changed, the same way as any other agent.

Nova isn't a model. The thinking is done by the provider she uses. Change it per discussion when you start one, or for good in her Provider and Quality Tier fields.

What an agent is made of

Every agent is a page in RedLeaf with these fields. Open an agent's page from a discussion's Info dialog, or from Database › Entities › Agent.

FieldWhat it's for
DescriptionOne line about what the agent does. Shown when you pick an agent for a new discussion.
AvatarIts picture.
IdentityIts personality, voice and behaviour, in plain words. This is the heart of the agent.
Output ProtocolRules it checks before every reply: tone, formatting, things to avoid.
ProtocolsShort conduct rules you can share between agents. See Skills and protocols.
SkillsReusable instruction packages it may use.
Provider, Quality TierWhich AI does its thinking by default, and at what quality: Fast, Standard, Deep or Research.
WorkspaceThe page its memory, configuration and files live under. Without one, the agent forgets everything between discussions.
Access levelWhat it may do without asking. Leave it on the default unless this agent needs something different. See Access levels.
Voice, Live VoiceHow it sounds when it speaks, and in voice mode. See Voice.
LiveGives it a standing Live discussion and a heartbeat. See Schedules and the heartbeat.
AutomationsThe schedules that run as this agent. Read only.

Create an agent

  1. Make its workspaceIn Workspace, choose New Project and name it after the agent. This page becomes its memory.
  2. Create the agentOpen Database, choose Entities, then Agent in the list of types. Under Instances, choose New.
  3. Give it a characterFill in its name, description and identity. Pick its provider and quality tier, and set Workspace to the page from step 1. Choose Create Entity.
  4. Talk to itStart a new discussion and pick it from the list.

An agent without a workspace still works, but the new-discussion list marks it Disposable storage · no cross-discussion memory.

Writing a good identity

Write it the way you'd brief a person. Who they are, what they're for, how they talk, and what they should never do. Be specific: “a patient German tutor who corrects one mistake at a time and answers in German unless I ask otherwise” works far better than “a helpful tutor”. Keep facts that change, like your progress or your preferences, out of the identity: the agent writes those into its memory itself.

{{icon:lightbulb}}

Edit the agent, not its files. An agent's workspace contains generated instruction files for its AI provider, such as CLAUDE.md and AGENTS.md. They are rebuilt from the agent's page, so edit the page and the files follow.

Several agents

Each discussion belongs to one agent and keeps its own history. With more than one agent, the Discussions sidebar can filter by agent. Agents can use different providers: a quick one for a quiz partner, a deep one for a research librarian.

Not sure what to make? The Learn recipe has a tutor, a coach and a sparring partner to start from.
