Transcript

Opening

Last time, you saw my workspace: what I remember, as pages you can open. But your lease, your receipts and your manuals aren't my memory. They're yours.

So they get a workspace of their own. Let's make one.

A new project

In the Workspace app, click New Project, and give it a name: Home paperwork. It's yours. You made it, and it isn't any agent's workspace.

It's on your disk

Now look at your disk. Next to Workspaces, there's a Projects folder, and Home paperwork is already in it. On Linux, it's under Leaf, in your home folder.

So drop in your scans, straight from your downloads. PDFs, photos, any kind of file.

Asking me

Then ask me to sort them. I read each file, move it into a folder by type, and write an index page with what matters: dates, deadlines, and anything I couldn't place.

The result

Back in Workspace, Home paperwork has its folders, and the index is a page, like any other. Edit it here, or as a file on your disk. It's the same page either way.

A week later

A week later, you ask when your lease renews. I read the index in your project, and answer from it.

Under the hood

So what changed underneath? Let's draw it.

Your project is a tree of pages, in the same database as my workspace, and every page keeps its history.

Files that aren't pages, like your scans, are kept in a file store next to the database, and your backups cover both.

On your disk, agents' workspaces sit under Workspaces, and the projects you make sit under Projects. Create, rename or delete one in RedLeaf, and its folder follows within a second.

One exception. Anything marked confidential stays off the drive, because any program on your computer can read those folders. You open it in RedLeaf only.

And when I work in your project, I read and write the same pages and files you do. My workspace stays mine, and your project stays yours.

Recap

So: your files go in a project of your own. It's a folder on your disk, under Projects, and pages in RedLeaf. And I work in it with you, on the same pages and files.
