Transcript

Opening

Thinking is half the job. The other half is reaching the things you work with.

So here's how I reach further, and how you stay in charge.

The ask

A client demo this morning, and you'd like the meeting room ready. I check what's installed. Nothing reaches that room yet, so I point you to the piece that does.

The Marketplace

That piece is Smart Home, an extension. Extensions add what RedLeaf doesn't do out of the box: an app, a connection to the devices you own, or a provider. Its card says whether it fits your machine, and its guide says what it does, what you'll need, and what it costs.

Installing is your call: I suggest, I never install. Once it's installed, it adds its own area to the list of things I can call, so I know how to use it. You link the lights and speakers once, and it's ready.

The room

Then I do it. I look up how to reach it, one call for the lights, one for the music, and the room is ready before anyone walks in.

The cover

One more thing: a cover for the deck. Image Generation is a capability, a shared slot that does nothing on its own until a provider fills it. Here, it's one that runs on your graphics card. Moments later, the cover is in the discussion.

Under the hood

So what's underneath? At the center is the core: RedLeaf, home of your agents and your data, and RedCompute, the engine that runs AI jobs. Everything else is an extension, and every one is signed.

Capabilities are the slots. Providers fill them, on your computer or in the cloud. When two fill the same one, you choose the default.

Everything the apps and I do goes through one local API, and it describes itself. I start from its map, and each extension you install adds an area. Every call I make carries a signed identity: who asked, and on whose behalf. You see each call as a square you can open, and every job says who started it.

And for the rest, I use the command-line tools already on your computer.

Recap

So: extensions widen what I can reach, and you decide what's installed. Capabilities are slots that providers fill. And every call I make is signed and on the record.

Next time: your own agents. See you there.
