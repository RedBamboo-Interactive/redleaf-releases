Transcript

Opening

Every team has a job that comes back after every meeting: keeping the handbook current.

So let's give that job an agent of its own, built from the same parts as me.

A home first

First, a home. In Workspace, make a new project, and name it after the agent: Sage. That project becomes its workspace.

The agent itself

Then the agent itself. In the Database app, every kind of item has its list. Open Agent, and choose New.

Give it a name and a line about its job, a face, and an identity, written the way you'd brief a new colleague.

Its workspace is the project we just made, and its skill is Meeting write-up, the same one I use. Which AI does its thinking is your choice. More on that next time.

Create it, then set its access level to Ask: it edits its own pages, and asks before anything else.

On your disk

On your disk, Sage has a folder of its own, right next to mine.

Working together

Now we share the work. After the planning call, tell me Sage keeps the handbook. I read the call notes, and open a discussion for Sage, with a brief. You don't carry messages between us.

Sage at work

Sage's discussion opens with my brief, marked as coming from me. It reads its write-up skill, and updates the handbook. To send me a note, it runs a command, so it asks first. Allow it once, and the note is on its way.

Under the hood

So what's underneath? Sage is a page, like me, made of the same parts. Only what's in them differs.

Each workspace is its own folder, with its own memory. Skills live in one library, and any agent can use them. Access is set per agent: I'm on the default, Sage asks. And each of us can use a different provider.

To share work, I open a discussion for Sage with a brief, and Sage answers with a note. Each discussion stays with its own agent.

Recap

So: a new agent has the same parts as me, and a job of its own. Skills are shared. Workspaces and access levels aren't. And we pass work between us, so you don't have to.
