Transcript

Opening

A plan is only useful if it's still there next month, and if someone actually remembers the dates.

Today: a real project, a plan that holds, and reminders I keep for you.

Talking it through

Say what you want in plain words, and which project it's for. I read what's already there. Your booking says you land on Thursday evening, so Friday is day one. I propose something concrete, not a list of options, and save it as a page in your project.

Dates are where plans fall apart. So ask for a reminder, and I schedule it myself.

The plan

Here it is in Workspace: a page, next to your bookings and tickets. It's yours to edit. Move Belém to the morning, and the next time we talk, I plan from your version.

The first of March

On the first of March, the reminder fires. I read the plan first, then I tell you in Live. Live is the one discussion that is always open between us, where I come to you first. With the context, not just a ping.

Under the hood

So what's underneath? The plan is a page in your project, on the same database as everything else.

The reminder is a schedule. It's mine, and it runs on your computer, only while RedLeaf is running. If RedLeaf was off when it was due, the schedule has a rule for the run it missed: skip it, or catch up once.

Every schedule runs a workflow, a small graph of steps you can open and read.

Each time it fires, that's a job, with when it ran, how long it took, and what it cost.

And the result comes to you, in the Live discussion.

Recap

So: plans are pages in your project. Reminders are schedules I own, on your computer. And when one fires, I tell you in Live, with the context.
