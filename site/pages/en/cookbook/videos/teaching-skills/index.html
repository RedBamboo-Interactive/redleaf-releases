Transcript

Opening

Every team has its way of doing things. You shouldn't have to explain it every week.

Show me once, and I'll keep doing it your way.

Monday's meeting

After the weekly meeting, you hand me the raw notes. I write them up my way. It reads fine, but your team wants it another way. So I rewrite it.

Make it a skill

Then you say: make that a skill. A skill is a procedure, written down once, that an agent picks up whenever it applies. I create it, and I give it to the team's other agent too, so we both work the same way.

The skill

It's a page in RedLeaf, like an agent. The description says when to use it. The instructions say how. And the Agents field shows who has it.

A week later

A week later, new meeting, new notes. Nothing else. I can tell the skill fits, so I open it, and the write-up comes out your way, without being asked.

One rule for everyone

Some rules are smaller than a procedure. Those are protocols. A protocol is a short rule of conduct, part of the instructions of every agent that has it. Like how to write a date, so nobody wonders which Friday.

Under the hood

So what's underneath? A skill is one page: a description written as a trigger, instructions in plain words, and any files it needs, like a template.

You give it to agents in their Skills field. RedLeaf puts a read-only copy in each agent's workspace, in the format its AI provider expects, and keeps it current when you edit the skill.

I read the descriptions first. When one fits the job, I open the whole skill.

Facts that change go in my memory. Procedures go in skills. Protocols are the short rules your agents share. And heartbeat skills are the same idea, for what my heartbeat may do on each tick.

Recap

So: show me once, then make it a skill. One skill works for every agent you give it to. Facts go in memory, procedures in skills, and shared rules in protocols.
