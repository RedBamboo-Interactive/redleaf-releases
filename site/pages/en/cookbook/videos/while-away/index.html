Transcript

Opening

You close the laptop at seven. The business keeps going.

So here's what I do while you're away, and what I'm not allowed to do.

Ask once

Tell me what to watch, in plain words. I turn it into automations, and I pick the kind. Watching a page for booth registration to open is a simple check: no AI, so it costs nothing. A weekly sales summary takes judgment, so that one is an agent check.

Early morning

Tuesday, six in the morning. Registration opens. The check catches it within minutes, and when you wake up, it's waiting for you in Live, with the early-bird deadline.

The heartbeat

Then there's my heartbeat. About once an hour, I look at what happened: your discussions, finished jobs, events from your apps. Most of the time, nothing needs doing. But on Monday's call, you promised Brightwell a quote by Thursday, and nothing has been drafted. So I write it down, and I start the draft, in its own discussion.

The nudge

On Wednesday, I tell you. The draft is ready, and it isn't sent. Sending it is your call.

Friday

And on Friday at four, the summary runs. I read the week's accounts, and you get what matters: what you sold, the big win, and the customer who has gone quiet.

Under the hood

So what's underneath? Each automation is a schedule, on your computer. A simple check calls a web address and compares the answer. An agent check runs a prompt through your AI provider. Every run is a job you can open.

The heartbeat is a regular tick too. What a tick may do comes from my heartbeat skills: action items, nudges, and initiatives.

Without your permission, the heartbeat does nothing outside your machine, nothing in your name, and nothing that spends money or destroys information. And since nobody is there to click Allow, a safety check approves the routine steps and blocks the risky ones.

Recap

So: simple checks cost nothing, agent checks think. What they find waits for you in Live. And the heartbeat notices, drafts, and never sends without you.
