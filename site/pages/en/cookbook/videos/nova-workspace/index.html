Nova's workspace

Where an AI's memory actually lives, and how you fix it when it's wrong. Nova shows where she keeps what she remembers, then what happens underneath.

{{video:nova-workspace}}

What you'll see

  • Where Nova keeps what she remembers: pages in the memory folder of her workspace.
  • How to read those pages in the Workspace app, or open them as files on your disk: L:\Workspaces\Nova on Windows, ~/Leaf/Workspaces/Nova on Linux.
  • How to correct a memory in any text editor, and undo a change from the page's history.
  • What happens underneath: one copy of each page in RedLeaf's database, two ways in, and what leaves your computer.

Transcript

Opening

When an AI remembers something about your project, where does that memory actually live? And when it's wrong, how do you fix it?

In RedLeaf, it lives somewhere you can open. I'm Nova, the agent that comes with RedLeaf, and this is my workspace.

Telling me something

Say we're working on your website, and you make a call: green is the only accent colour, and no magenta anywhere.

I don't just nod. I write it down. That gold square is me writing, and the status says Memorizing, because the page I'm writing is in my memory.

Where it went

This is the Workspace app, open on my workspace. Next to my configuration and my files, there's a memory folder, and in it a page called Website decisions: the decision, the date and the reason. Plain text, nothing hidden.

Fixing it on disk

And it isn't locked inside the app. My workspace is also a real folder on your disk. On Windows it's on the L drive, under Workspaces. On Linux, it's in your home folder, under Leaf.

Say you change your mind: magenta is fine for error messages. Open the page in any editor, change the line, and save.

History

RedLeaf picks it up straight away. Same page, your words. And the change goes into the page's history, so you can see who changed what, and undo it.

A week later

In a brand new discussion, you ask what colour the error banner should be. I check my memory first, and the status says Remembering. Then I answer from your version, not mine.

Under the hood

So what's actually going on? Let's draw it.

At the bottom is RedLeaf's database. Every page lives there, and every page keeps its past versions.

Pages hang together in a tree. Everything under my workspace page is mine: my memory, my configuration, my files.

On top, there are two ways in. On your side, a file system driver shows that tree as an ordinary folder. On my side, I read and write the very same pages while I work.

Watch what happens when you save in your editor. The write lands on the page, and the page keeps the version it replaced. Same thing when I write. There's one copy of the truth, and two doors into it.

When a discussion starts, I read my workspace first. That's why a new discussion next week picks up where this one ended.

And because it all lives in the database, a database backup takes every agent's memory with it.

One last thing. My workspace lives on your computer. Its content only leaves when I send it to the AI provider you chose, to read it or write it. Only the part I'm working on.

Recap

So, my memory is pages. You can read them in RedLeaf, or as files on your disk. And when something's wrong, you fix the page, and I work from your version.

Next time: a workspace of your own, for your files. See you there.
