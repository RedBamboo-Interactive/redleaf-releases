RedLeaf, from the first hello to the local API.

RedLeaf is a desktop suite for Windows and Linux where your AI agents work alongside you. It gives them apps, tools and a memory that lasts, and shows you every step they take. These pages cover installing it, using it day to day, and the parts experts reach for.

Start here

Install on WindowsDownload the launcher, approve WinFsp once, and open RedLeaf in your browser. Install on LinuxInstall FUSE 3, run the one-line installer as yourself, and open RedLeaf. Set up RedLeafThe six setup chapters: your account, your AI provider and your first hello. Your first conversationWhat to ask Nova first, and how to read what she does.

How RedLeaf fits together

You talk to agents. Nova is the agent that comes with RedLeaf, and you can add your own. An agent's thinking is done by the AI provider you choose for it, such as Claude Code or Codex. RedLeaf itself is not a model: it is the place where the agents live, keep their memory, use apps and report back.

PartWhat it does
NovaThe chat app. Discussions with your agents, by text or by voice, plus their Live timeline and Journal.
CodeCoding sessions on your projects, on their own branches if you like, with GitHub issues, reviews and tests.
WorkspaceProjects and pages. Each agent's memory is a set of pages here, and also a real folder on your disk.
DatabaseEvery entity in RedLeaf, its types and fields, and one search across all of it.
ComputeThe engine that runs AI jobs. Shows every job, who asked for it and what it cost.
SettingsYour account, access levels, updates, storage and backups, remote access and extensions.

Open any app from the app switcher at the top left of RedLeaf. Extensions can add more apps, such as Smart Home or Ambience.
