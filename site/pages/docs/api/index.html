Going further

The local API.

Everything the apps do goes through a local API, and so does everything your agents do. It describes itself, so an agent can find the right call without guessing, and every call carries a signed identity that says who asked and for whom.

Two services

ServiceAddressWhat it does
RedLeafhttp://127.0.0.1:18804Entities, apps, extensions, discussions, automations, settings, authentication
RedComputehttp://127.0.0.1:18800The AI engine: sessions, inference, speech, images and other jobs

Both listen only on your own machine. Remote access goes through RedLeaf. The ports are shown in Settings › System and in the AI Compute settings.

Discovery

Start with /discover on either service. It's a short map of areas, each with its routes, schemas and concepts. Search it, or open one area:

GET /discover                      the map
GET /discover/find?q=automations   search it
GET /discover/automations          one area in full
GET /openapi.json                  the OpenAPI document

Extensions add their own areas, so what you see depends on what's installed. That's the point: an agent reads discovery for the live contract instead of relying on a route it remembers.

Authentication

Every call needs a token, sent as Authorization: Bearer <token>. Discovery says "authMode": "Required"; a route that lists no auth of its own is still not public.

  • The apps in your browser mint short-lived execution tokens from your session, for one app at a time.
  • Agents get one in the REDLEAF_EXECUTION_TOKEN environment variable of their session.
curl -H "Authorization: Bearer $REDLEAF_EXECUTION_TOKEN" \
  http://127.0.0.1:18804/auth/execution-context

/auth/execution-context returns who the token speaks for: the app, the actor, the beneficiary, the discussion it came from, its parent, and when it expires. A token never grants more than the person it acts for is allowed.

{{icon:shield-check}}

Tokens stay on the machine. Send an execution token only to the local RedLeaf and RedCompute. Never put it in a URL, a log or a browser's storage, and never send it to another website. The skills that ship with Nova follow these rules; follow them in yours.

Every call is on the record

Because each token is signed, RedLeaf knows which app, agent and discussion every call came from. In Compute › Jobs, each job shows its origin, its actor and its beneficiary, its timeline and its cost, and you can filter by any of them. That's how you answer “who started this, and why?” for anything that ran.

Writing your own tools

The simplest way to automate RedLeaf is to ask an agent: it already holds a signed token, reads discovery, and shows its calls as step squares you can check. For anything an agent should do the same way every time, write it down as a skill.
